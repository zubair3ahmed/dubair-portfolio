Design decisions and trade-offs

Eight calls that shaped the product, and what each one cost.

1 · One question at a time

Dense forms dropped into a chat bubble felt overwhelming when I reviewed the reference flow. One question and one control per turn lowered the load.

GainedCalm, scannable, easy to resume
Gave upMore turns. I recovered them with decision 2

2 · AI proposes whole sections

Share capital, allocation, financials and office open with a pre-filled card. One click accepts it, and every value is revalidated.

Gained59 → 30 interactions in my prototype
Gave upRisk of blind acceptance. "Suggested" tags and the final review offset it

3 · Friction where the risk lives

Restricted activities need a deliberate declaration, ticked after reading the restrictions. Submitting has a declaration and a 5-second cancel window. Everything else stays light.

GainedAuditable consent where regulators care
Gave upA few extra seconds on the riskiest steps

4 · Verify before the form opens

Anyone could start an application. A short, conversational gate confirms identity and powers autosave and resume by email.

GainedFewer fake starts, and a way back in
Gave upSome people leave before seeing value. Code auto-continues to reduce this

5 · Flag only what is uncertain

The AI reads a passport, shows the reading steps, and only questions the blurry field. Confirming that field and choosing a role happens in one tap.

GainedTrust is calibrated: users check what matters
Gave upOccasional over-flagging until confidence thresholds are tuned on real data

6 · Bilingual by default

The registry needs every company name in Arabic too. The AI suggests it from the English name and detects the entity type, then asks the applicant to check it.

GainedNo hunting for a translator, one tap to confirm
Gave upTransliteration can be wrong, so it is always labelled "please check"

7 · Pay inside the assistant, not in a portal

Applicants used to log into a separate portal to pay, the last place you want to lose someone. The agent now prepares an itemised invoice, explains every line, and takes payment in the panel. Card, bank transfer or pay later. The card is only held, then charged after the 5-second cancel window.

GainedNo hand-off at the final step, and a receipt in the outcome card
Gave upMore trust needed. Card details stay with the payment partner, never the AI, and a decline gets a clear recovery path

8 · Two agents, one panel

The brief asked for a form agent and a consulting AI. I kept them in one panel with two tabs, so a question never costs your place in the form. The consultant cites its source and offers a person when it is not sure.

GainedAnswers without leaving the form, and one place to learn the rules
Gave upA narrower consultant: general guidance only, no legal advice, so some questions go to a person

Validation plan

How I would validate the riskiest assumptions.

Scope. I delivered the research, AI logic, design system and working prototype as part of a consulting engagement. Launch and user validation sat outside my scope, so this is my plan for validating it, not results.

The tests I would run with 6–8 first-time founders per round.

A · Do people blindly accept suggestions?

Hypothesis
Users still catch a wrong default.
Method
Seed one wrong suggestion, watch for corrections.
Metric
Blind-accept rate on the seeded error. Aim: under 10%.

B · Is the compliance gate understood?

Hypothesis
Users can restate what they agreed to.
Method
Post-task recall question on one restriction.
Metric
Correct recall. Aim: 80% or more.

C · Does the gate hurt completion?

Hypothesis
Verifying first lowers fake starts without lowering completions.
Method
A/B: gate first vs gate before submit.
Metric
Completion rate, fake-start rate, time to first answer.

D · One question vs dense card

Hypothesis
One question per turn is faster and has fewer errors.
Method
Within-subject task, counterbalanced.
Metric
Time, validation errors, post-task ease score.

After launch, I would track: goal completion, intent resolution speed, clarification efficiency, trust calibration, undo and escalation rates. Next step: Arabic and right-to-left layouts.
