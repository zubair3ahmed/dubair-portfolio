AI product design · consulting project

An AI assistant that makes a legal form feel like a conversation.

Applicants were dropping off halfway through a long manual form. I designed an AI assistant that does most of the typing and deciding for them, plus the rules for what it may never do alone. Every legal check and consent the regulator needs stays in place. In my prototype the happy path drops from 59 to 30 interactions (scripted runs, not user data).

Audit of the first build

The dev team had already shipped a version. I audited it, then redesigned the whole experience.

The first build was a chat widget docked beside the existing portal. It worked end to end, but it carried the form's problems into a narrower space. These are five of the issues I found, each next to what replaced it.

1A form dropped into a chat bubble

2The transcript kept growing

3Recommendations with no reasons

4A wall of legal text in a narrow panel

5Problems surfaced at the very end

Also found in the audit

  • The widget sat at about 390px wide over the full portal form, so two versions of the same form competed for attention.
  • "Start over" was the primary button in a popover that warned all progress would be cleared.
  • Enabled Confirm buttons used a pale purple that read as disabled.
  • The AI behaviour had no visible rules: nothing said what it could do alone, when it would ask, or when a person takes over.

AI logic & guidelines

The AI is a collaborator with a short leash, not a chatbot.

I used an AI-experience framework to decide what the AI may do, when it must ask, and when it must hand over. Domain risk for a regulated business registry is high, which set the defaults.

AssistPlain-language answers with a source line
RecommendActivities and section defaults, ranked, with reasons
CreateReads a passport or profile and drafts the fields
ActOnly submits after a declaration and cancel window
EscalateHands your progress to a person when unsure
1IntentOne question per turn. "What I understood" echo.
2Context"Based on your earlier answers". Draft resumes.
3PlanReading steps shown, never just "Thinking…"
4ActionsReads and writes look different. Writes need consent.
5ControlStop, undo, edit, cancel, talk to a person.
6TransparencyMatch %, reasons, restrictions, activity log.
7Outcome & recoveryResult card. Failure cards with next steps.

Where my judgment set the defaults

  • Autonomy level 4 stays unused. The ladder has a fully autonomous rung. In a legal filing I left it empty.
  • High confidence still asks. A confident match does not skip the declaration. Submit and pay stay at confirm.
  • Escalate, don't guess. An unknown legal question goes to a person, with the applicant's progress attached.
  • The AI never touches card details. They stay with the payment partner, and the card is held, not charged, until the cancel window ends.

Failure and recovery

When it cannot read a document, it says why and what is safe.

  • It says why. The passport expired on 12 Mar 2024, and registries need 6 more months.
  • It protects what exists. Nothing from the file is saved, and the other answers are untouched.
  • It offers three ways forward. Upload another, type the details, or talk to a person.
Design principles

Five rules every screen was held to.

They came from the problem, the risk of a regulated filing, and the AI logic above. Each decision below traces back to one of them.

1

Ask less, understand more

Every question has to earn its place. The AI reads documents and proposes answers instead of asking for them.

Decisions 1, 2
2

Friction only where the risk lives

Be fast everywhere except the few moments a regulator cares about. There, slow the user down on purpose.

Decisions 3, 4
3

Say what you are unsure of

Flag the blurry field, label a guess “please check”, show the reasons, and hand over to a person when unsure. Never guess in a legal process.

Decisions 5, 6, 8, 9
4

Nothing happens silently

The AI never submits, pays or removes anything without consent, a log entry and a way back. The top rung of autonomy stays unused.

Decisions 3, 7
5

Keep people in the flow

Pay, ask and fix without leaving the panel, so no step costs the applicant their place.

Decisions 7, 8

01
Principle 1 · Ask less, understand more

Ask one thing at a time.

Instead ofA dense card of fields
NowOne question, one control

The problem

Dense forms dropped into a chat bubble felt overwhelming in the reference flow I reviewed.

Why it works

One question and one control per turn lowers the load. Each answer is a clear step, so the flow is calm, scannable and easy to resume.

What it cost

More turns. Decision 2 wins them back.

02
Principle 1 · Ask less, understand more

Let the AI propose whole sections.

Instead ofEvery field typed by hand
NowA pre-filled card, one click (59 → 30)

The problem

Share capital, allocation, financials and the office each meant a screen of fields.

Why it works

Each opens with a pre-filled card. One click accepts it, and every value is revalidated, so speed never skips a check.

What it cost

Risk of blind acceptance. “Suggested” tags and the final review offset it.

03
Principle 2 · Friction only where the risk lives

Put the friction where the risk lives.

Instead ofThe same effort on every step
NowHeavy only where the risk is

The problem

A legal filing has a few moments a regulator cares about, and most of the form is not one of them.

Why it works

Restricted activities need a declaration, ticked after reading the restrictions. Submitting adds a declaration and a 5-second cancel window. Everything else stays light, so the heavy steps read as heavy.

What it cost

A few extra seconds on the riskiest steps.

04
Principle 2 · Friction only where the risk lives

Verify before the form opens.

Instead ofAnyone could start, no way back
NowVerified first, resume by email

The problem

Anyone could start an application, so there was no way back in and no reason to save progress.

Why it works

A short, conversational gate confirms identity and powers autosave and resume by email. The code continues on its own when complete.

What it cost

Some people leave before they see value. Auto-continue reduces this.

05
Principle 3 · Say what you are unsure of

Flag only what is uncertain.

Instead ofChecking every field the AI read
NowChecking only the uncertain one

The problem

If the AI asks about everything it read, checking is as slow as typing.

Why it works

The AI reads the passport, shows its reading steps, and questions only the blurry number. Confirming that field and choosing a role is one tap, so trust goes where it is needed.

What it cost

Occasional over-flagging until thresholds are tuned on real data.

06
Principle 3 · Say what you are unsure of

Bilingual by default.

Instead ofTyping or translating the name
NowSuggested, then checked

The problem

The registry needs every company name in Arabic as well as English.

Why it works

The AI suggests the Arabic name from the English one, detects the entity type, and asks the applicant to check it. No hunting for a translator, one tap to confirm.

What it cost

Transliteration can be wrong, so it is always labelled “please check”.

07
Principle 5 · Keep people in the flow

Pay inside the assistant, not in a portal.

Instead ofA separate payment portal
NowPay in the assistant panel

The problem

Applicants used to log into a separate portal to pay, the last place you want to lose someone.

Why it works

The agent prepares an itemised invoice, explains every line, and takes payment in the panel: card, bank transfer or pay later. The card is only held, then charged after the cancel window, and the receipt lands in the outcome card.

What it cost

More trust needed. Card details stay with the payment partner, never the AI, and a decline gets a clear recovery path.

08
Principle 5 · Keep people in the flow

Two agents, one panel.

Instead ofLeaving the form to ask
NowAsk in a second tab

The problem

The brief asked for a form agent and a consulting AI. Two products would mean leaving the form to ask.

Why it works

I kept them in one panel with two tabs. The consultant cites its source and offers a person when it is not sure, so you learn the rules without losing your place.

What it cost

A narrower consultant: general guidance only, no legal advice, so some questions go to a person.

09
Principle 3 · Say what you are unsure of

Show the reasons, not just the answer.

Instead ofSearching a long list
NowRanked, with reasons

The problem

Applicants used to search long activity lists, and a recommendation with no reason is just a guess.

Why it works

Each suggestion shows a match percentage, its activity code and a View restrictions link. The applicant can add it, add the top three, or dismiss it, and still search everything.

What it cost

A score can look more certain than it is, so the restrictions stay one tap away and must be acknowledged.

Validation plan

How I would validate the riskiest assumptions.

Scope. I delivered the research, AI logic, design system and working prototype as part of a consulting engagement. Launch and user validation sat outside my scope, so this is my plan for validating it, not results.

The tests I would run with 6–8 first-time founders per round.

A · Do people blindly accept suggestions?

Hypothesis
Users still catch a wrong default.
Method
Seed one wrong suggestion, watch for corrections.
Metric
Blind-accept rate on the seeded error. Aim: under 10%.

B · Is the compliance gate understood?

Hypothesis
Users can restate what they agreed to.
Method
Post-task recall question on one restriction.
Metric
Correct recall. Aim: 80% or more.

C · Does the gate hurt completion?

Hypothesis
Verifying first lowers fake starts without lowering completions.
Method
A/B: gate first vs gate before submit.
Metric
Completion rate, fake-start rate, time to first answer.

D · One question vs dense card

Hypothesis
One question per turn is faster and has fewer errors.
Method
Within-subject task, counterbalanced.
Metric
Time, validation errors, post-task ease score.

After launch, I would track: goal completion, intent resolution speed, clarification efficiency, trust calibration, undo and escalation rates. Next step: Arabic and right-to-left layouts.
