The Challenge

Build one app that feels native in two directions

Saudi Arabia's ride-hailing market is uniquely bilingual. Users switch between Arabic and English mid-session. The challenge was not just translating strings, but rethinking every interaction, from map overlays to swipe gestures, so the app feels native in both RTL and LTR without maintaining two separate codebases.

The existing ride-hailing apps in the region treated Arabic as an afterthought: mirrored layouts with broken alignment, icons pointing the wrong way, and typography that didn't breathe. We set out to design RTL-first.

Core Flow

From "Where to?" to confirmed pickup

Contextual Home Screen

The home screen is a full-bleed map with a bottom sheet. We personalize the greeting ("Where to, Noura?") and surface the last destination, favourite drivers nearby, and saved places. In Arabic, the greeting reads "إلى أين تود الذهاب؟" with the search field aligned right.


Smart Destination Search

As the user types, we show distance-sorted suggestions with recognizable icons: office buildings, parks, airports. The Arabic keyboard layout works seamlessly with the search field, and results use Arabic numerals for distance (٤٫٢ كم).

Arabic Experience

Search in Arabic, natively

RTL-First Input Design

The destination search in Arabic mirrors the entire input paradigm. The search icon moves to the right, the clear button to the left, and suggested destinations list with Arabic numerals. Location types show culturally relevant icons.


Arabic Keyboard Integration

The search bar receives Arabic input with proper kerning and ligature rendering. We tested with real Saudi location names (مركز الملك عبدالله المالي, مطار الملك خالد الدولي) to ensure truncation never cuts mid-ligature.

Fare & Vehicle

Choose your ride, see the price

Transparent Fare Estimates

Three vehicle tiers (Economy, Comfort, XL) with real-time pricing in SAR. Each option shows seat count, estimated wait time, and a clear price. The selected vehicle gets a purple highlight with the CTA updating dynamically: "Choose Economy · SAR 48".


Confirm Your Pickup

After selecting a vehicle, the map zooms to the pickup zone with a route line to the destination. The bottom sheet shows the exact address, allows pickup notes for the driver, and a single-tap confirmation button.

Mirrored Components

Every screen, every direction

The component system uses auto-layout with direction tokens. When the language switches, padding, icon positions, and text alignment all flip automatically. No manual overrides needed.

RTL Design Principles

What we learned designing RTL

↔

Mirror, Don't Flip

Not everything should flip in RTL. Maps stay geographic (north is north). Progress bars that represent time move left-to-right universally. Play/media controls keep their global convention.

🔤

Typography Rhythm

Arabic script is inherently wider and needs more line height. We use 1.7x line height for Arabic vs 1.5x for English, and adjusted font sizes to maintain visual weight parity.

📍

Map UI Positioning

Map controls (zoom, recenter) move from right to left in RTL. The pickup pin, route line, and driver marker are directionally neutral. Location names render in the active language.

🔢

Number Systems

Saudi users expect Eastern Arabic numerals (١٢٣) in Arabic mode but Western digits (123) in English. Prices always show in SAR with the correct numeral system for the active language.

Live Trip

Driver on the way, ride in progress

Real-Time Driver Tracking

Once matched, the rider sees the driver's real-time position with an ETA countdown. The driver card shows name, photo, rating, car details, and a bilingual license plate (both Arabic and Western characters).


Meet at the Pickup Point

A countdown timer anchors the meet-up screen. Pickup and destination addresses are clearly labelled. Trip details (vehicle type, distance) and payment info are visible. Two primary actions: confirm arrival or cancel.

Signature Feature

Favourite driver nearby

Ride With Drivers You Trust

A unique feature that surfaces when a previously-favourited driver is nearby. The rider sees the driver's name, rating, car, distance, and ETA. One tap to request them specifically, creating a relationship layer that ride-hailing apps typically lack.


Cultural Context

In Saudi Arabia, trust and familiarity are central to service experiences. The favourite driver feature respects this by letting riders build a preferred driver network, especially important for family members who may prefer a known driver.
