01 / My Contribution

My contribution

I led product design for XBert's onboarding and support workflows, partnering with product, research, design, and engineering over eight months. My focus was the owner's journey: defining the AI's identity, assigning skills, configuring coverage, and testing behavior before going live. The broader service journey connected routine automation with escalation to staff.

02 / Context & Problem

The busiest hours were the hardest to answer.

Casa Dorado Restaurant Group operated 14 locations: four fine-dining restaurants, seven casual-dining restaurants, and three fast-casual outlets. Reservations, takeout orders, catering inquiries, and everyday questions arrived in English and Spanish while staff were serving guests.

The project's customer snapshot reported that 41% of inbound inquiries went unanswered. Every missed call could mean a lost reservation, an abandoned order, or another interruption for the team trying to call back.

For the customer

A simple question or reservation depended on someone being free to pick up. During peak service, the next step could become voicemail or trying another restaurant.

For the restaurant

The same staff had to look after guests and answer the phone. Growing demand stretched that operating model across every location.

How might we respond to customers immediately — without adding more work for the people serving them?

XBert needed to handle routine requests and involve staff when needed. Just as importantly, owners needed a way to set it up and manage it without technical expertise.

03 / Research & Priorities

Discovery methods and their purpose

Discovery combined a 30-day call audit, host-stand shadowing, guest interviews, and competitive analysis. Together, these methods connected call patterns with operational bottlenecks and customer expectations.

Research Method What it helped us understand
30-day call audit Call volume and patterns: when demand arrived and where coverage broke down.
Host-stand shadowing How phone calls competed with in-person service during busy periods.
Guest interviews What customers expected when contacting the restaurant.
Competitive analysis How existing solutions approached customer communication.

Three insights shaped the priorities.

01 / Timing

Peak demand created peak failure.

The dinner rush brought the highest call volume at the same time staff were busiest with guests.

Design response: Make call coverage configurable so routine inquiries do not depend on staff availability.
02 / Repetition

Much of the work was predictable.

Table availability, reservations, opening hours, and takeout requests repeated across calls.

Design response: Organize automation around recognizable business skills with clear responsibilities.
03 / Language

Coverage had to work in two languages.

English and Spanish were part of the restaurant group's everyday service needs.

Design response: Make language a visible setup choice alongside the AI's voice and greeting.
Product Requirement

Owners needed confidence before launch.

The brief called for setup and management without technical expertise.

Design response: Put testing close to configuration, with responses and actions available to inspect.

The priority: protect staff attention. Answer routine questions, support both languages, and make escalation part of the service.

04 / End-to-End Solution

Make the AI's responsibilities visible before it goes live.

The onboarding experience breaks the work into understandable choices: how XBert represents the business, what it can do, and when it answers. These three decisions connect the restaurant's needs to the product screens.

01

Identity Before Automation

Voice · Language · Greeting

Make the AI's behavior a business choice. The first setup step brings voice, languages, personality, and greeting together. A business can choose how the AI represents it before assigning responsibilities.

02

Capabilities with Boundaries

Skills · Dependencies · Catalog

Describe work as skills, not an open-ended promise. The skill picker presents tasks owners recognize — FAQs, appointments, takeout orders. Required apps are shown on the cards so a capability's dependencies are visible when selected.

03

Confidence Before Launch

Test · Inspect · Schedule

Let owners inspect behavior while configuring coverage. The final setup view places inbox, schedule, and call-answering settings beside a chat test. Owners see the customer-facing reply and inspect the AI's actions in a separate panel.

Two connected journeys: the owner and the caller.

The owner configures the service; the customer experiences it in a conversation. Keeping those journeys connected makes coverage, capabilities, and escalation part of the same product story.

The Caller: Get an Answer and a Next Step

Autonomy with a defined scope. XBert handles routine work; owners define its responsibilities and coverage. Complex requests have a path to staff.

05 / Testing & Validation

Make behavior testable before customers depend on it.

The pre-launch experience supports phone and chat testing, suggested questions, and an owner-only view of AI actions. That lets an owner examine more than whether a reply sounds natural — they can check whether XBert used the expected skill and business information.

Proposed validation scenarios

The following is a proposed validation plan, organized around the moments most likely to affect trust and service quality.

Scenario to test Success signal
Owner sets busy-hour or after-hours coverage They can explain when XBert will answer and correctly configure that behavior without help.
Caller asks a routine question in English or Spanish The response uses the intended language and the correct business information.
A booking lookup fails or an integration is unavailable The conversation communicates the failure and offers a useful next step.
A request requires staff, but nobody is available The caller knows what happens next, and the request can be recovered with its context.
The owner spots an incorrect answer They can find the relevant setting or knowledge, correct it, and retest the behavior.

06 / AI Components

Component design from Figma

The design system behind XBert's onboarding was built as a modular component library in Figma. Each screen composes from shared primitives — avatar pickers, skill cards, channel toggles, and testing interfaces — so the flow stays consistent as features grow.

07 / Creating Your AI Agent

From sign-up to live agent in minutes.

The onboarding flow guides business owners through a step-by-step process to create and deploy their AI agent — from account setup and business details to voice configuration, knowledge review, testing, and channel assignment. The goal: make a complex AI product feel approachable, even for non-technical users.

Account & Business Setup

Onboarding begins with account creation and collecting core business information — company name, industry, address, and operating hours. This data seeds the AI agent's initial knowledge base so it can answer basic questions from day one.

Voice & Personality Configuration

Owners choose a voice, set the language and greeting style, and configure how the AI introduces itself. This step shapes the agent's personality — turning a generic bot into a branded experience that matches the business's tone.

AI Knowledge Review

Before going live, the system presents an AI-generated summary of what it has learned about the business. Owners review and correct this knowledge, ensuring the agent's responses are accurate and reflect their actual services, policies, and FAQs.

Testing & Preview

A built-in test environment lets owners preview how the AI agent handles real conversations — via voice call or chat — before any customer interaction. This builds confidence and catches misconfigurations early.

Channel Configuration & Go-Live

The final step assigns the AI agent to communication channels — phone, SMS, or web chat. Owners configure which channels the agent handles, set up call forwarding rules, and activate the agent. Once live, the dashboard provides real-time monitoring.

Management & Inbox

Post-launch, the management view lets owners monitor all AI employee instances, while the General Inbox aggregates every conversation the agent has handled — giving full visibility into what the AI is saying on behalf of the business.

09 / Reflection

What I take forward

Design the owner's confidence, too.

A natural conversation is only one part of an AI service. Clear setup, explicit responsibilities, and inspectable actions help the business understand what it is putting in front of customers.

Treat response time as part of the service.

For a restaurant, availability affects reservations and staff attention. The design has to connect customer convenience with the practical rhythm of the business.

Make correction and recovery the next priority.

I would deepen the experience for correcting business knowledge, recovering from failed actions, and receiving escalations. Those moments determine whether staff can rely on the service.
